# Abstract
Atmospheric rivers (ARs) are elongated corridors of intense water vapour transport that can produce extreme precipitation and flooding. Although their role has been widely studied in mid-latitude coastal regions, their contribution to major floods across global river basins remains less well quantified. Here, we assess the association between ARs and 119 major flood events recorded across 50 representative river basins worldwide during 1999–2018. We evaluated the spatial and temporal coincidence between ARs and flood-affected areas, entire river basins, and sub-basins. In addition, we analysed AR activity during flood periods and up to three days before flood onset to assess both direct triggering and antecedent preconditioning effects. The results show that ARs intersected 74% of the flood-affected areas and were present in 89% of the corresponding river basins. AR-related precipitation was especially relevant during the flood period and the day immediately preceding flood onset, suggesting that ARs can act both as direct flood triggers and as preconditioning mechanisms by increasing antecedent wetness. AR-associated floods were also linked to substantial societal impacts, including fatalities and population displacement.
