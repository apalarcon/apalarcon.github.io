# Abstact
North-Eastern South America (NES) is a tropical hydroclimatic hotspot where recurrent droughts affect 
ecosystems, reservoirs, river discharge and the water availability required to sustain socioeconomic
activities. In recent decades, parts of NES have experienced marked agricultural growth supported by 
irrigation infrastructure and water-management strategies. This expansion increases the importance of 
understanding drought variability and the atmospheric processes controlling regional moisture supply. Here 
we analyse monthly Standardised Precipitation Index (SPI) and the Standardised Precipitation–
Evapotranspiration Index (SPEI) series at 1-, 3-, 6- and 12-month accumulation scales to characterise 
drought variability, persistence and trends. Drought episodes were defined as continuous negative phases 
reaching at least moderate intensity (≤ −0.84) and ending when positive values returned. To assess the 
physical role of moisture supply, we identified atmospheric moisture sources using a Lagrangian moisture-tracking framework based on FLEXPART, driven by ERA5, and analysed spatial anomalies in source contributions during contrasting dry and wet conditions. The results show a significant drying in both SPI 
and SPEI, with stronger trends in SPEI, indicating that evaporative demand intensifies precipitation deficit
effects. SPEI trends vary from −0.19 decade⁻¹ at 1 month to −0.35 decade⁻¹ at 12 months, while SPI trends 
range from −0.15 to −0.29 decade⁻¹. Additionally, moisture-source diagnostics identify the tropical North 
and South Atlantic as the dominant oceanic sources for NES precipitation, together with regional terrestrial 
recycling, which is less important. During dry conditions, moisture uptake weakens over the Atlantic source 
regions, particularly in the western part, from 0º to 40ºS. The most persistent region associated with drought 
episodes was finally identified at the seasonal scale. These changes are further supported by anomalies in the 
vertically integrated moisture flux, confirming variations in the Atlantic moisture inflow towards NES.
